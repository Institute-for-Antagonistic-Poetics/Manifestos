A cut-up machine for sound, after Burroughs, Gysin & Sommerville. Hard cuts only, unless you lose your nerve.

3. The cut-up

Red marks are audible splices. Seams where two neighbouring pieces happen to land back in their original order heal over and are not marked.

Provenance map. Each line is one piece of tape. Across: when you hear it. Up: where it was on the original. The dashed diagonal is the tape played straight through. A line sloping down was played backwards; a steeper line was played faster; scattered dashes are a cut-up.

4. Recipe

Everything needed to remake this cut-up from the same tape. Copy it to share; paste one in and press Apply to restore it.

About, keys, and fine print

Keys: space play/stop the cut-up · r reroll the seed.

Pipeline, in order: cut into pieces → rearrange → flip some → set speeds → splice. Each step draws on its own stream of random numbers, so turning one knob never reshuffles the others: raising "flip pieces" only ever adds flips, it never chooses different ones.

Everything runs in this page; nothing is uploaded. Stereo files are mixed to mono, and tapes are trimmed to 5 minutes. If the Record button is refused when this file is opened directly, serve the folder instead (python3 -m http.server) and open http://localhost:8000/tape.html.

Cut up your own voice. Cutting up other people's voices is how you put words in their mouths. That was Burroughs's point, and it is also how a deepfake works.
